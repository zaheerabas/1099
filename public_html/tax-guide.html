The two taxes you pay

When you work for yourself, no one withholds tax from your pay. You owe two federal taxes on your profit:

  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% of 92.35% of your net profit. That’s 12.4% for Social Security, up to a 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus 2.9% for Medicare with no limit.
  • Federal income tax: the same brackets everyone uses, applied to your income after deductions.

Half of your self-employment tax is deductible from your income, which lowers your income tax a little. An extra 0.9% Medicare tax applies above $200,000 of earnings ($250,000 married filing jointly).

Deductions that lower your tax

Business expenses lower both of your taxes, so each dollar you deduct is worth more than it would be for an employee.

  • Ordinary business expenses: software, equipment, supplies, phone and internet (the business share), advertising, platform fees, and professional services.
  • Vehicle: 72.5¢ per business mile from January to June 2026 and 76¢ from July 1. Commuting to a regular workplace doesn’t count.
  • Home office: if you use part of your home regularly and only for work, the simplified method gives $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet.
  • Health insurance: premiums you pay yourself, when you can’t join an employer plan, are deductible from income.
  • Retirement: SEP-IRA and solo 401(k) contributions lower your income tax.
  • Qualified business income deduction: up to 20% of your business income. It’s now permanent, and from 2026 there’s a $400 minimum if your business income is at least $1,000. Extra limits start above $201,750 of taxable income ($403,500 joint).

You owe tax on all your business income, whether or not you get a form for it. With the higher 2026 thresholds, many smaller clients won’t send one, so keep your own record of what you’re paid.

Quarterly estimated payments

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more after withholding, the IRS expects you to pay during the year using Form 1040-ES or IRS Direct Pay.

PaymentCovers income earnedDue date
1stJanuary 1 to March 31, 2026April 15, 2026
2ndApril 1 to May 31, 2026June 15, 2026
3rdJune 1 to August 31, 2026September 15, 2026
4thSeptember 1 to December 31, 2026January 15, 2027

To avoid an underpayment penalty, pay at least the smaller of 90% of your 2026 tax or 100% of your 2025 tax through withholding and estimated payments. If your 2025 adjusted gross income was over $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately), use 110% of your 2025 tax.

Records to keep

  • Every 1099 you receive, plus your own list of payments from clients who didn’t send one.
  • Receipts or statements for business expenses.
  • A mileage log with date, purpose and miles, split before and after July 1, 2026.
  • Confirmation of each estimated payment you make.

Keep them for at least three years after you file.
