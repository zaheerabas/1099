How the 1099 calculator works

Every number the calculator shows, step by step, with a worked example for $60,000 of freelance income.

Updated October 2026 for tax year 2026

Freelancers pay two federal taxes on their business profit: self-employment tax and income tax. The calculator works them out in the same order as the IRS forms, Schedule C, Schedule SE and Form 1040.

1. Business profit

We add your 1099-NEC, 1099-K and 1099-MISC income, then subtract your business expenses, your home office and your mileage.

  • Home office uses the simplified method: $5 per square foot for up to 300 square feet. It can bring your profit down to zero but not below.
  • Mileage uses 72.5¢ a mile from January to June and 76¢ from July 1, after the IRS raised the rate mid-year.
  • 1099-INT interest isn’t business income. It’s taxed as ordinary income but not for self-employment tax.

2. Self-employment tax

Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax an employer would normally split with you. It’s 15.3% of 92.35% of your profit: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare.

  • The 12.4% part only applies up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Any W-2 wages count toward that limit first.
  • If your net earnings are under $400, there’s no self-employment tax.
  • An extra 0.9% Medicare tax applies to earnings above $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly, $125,000 married filing separately).

3. Income that’s taxed

Your adjusted gross income is your profit plus any W-2 wages and interest, minus three deductions: half of your self-employment tax, health insurance premiums you paid yourself, and SEP-IRA or solo 401(k) contributions.

From that we subtract the 2026 standard deduction and the 20% qualified business income deduction. For most freelancers the business deduction is 20% of profit (after those adjustments), but never more than 20% of taxable income. From 2026 there is also a $400 minimum if your business income is at least $1,000.

4. Federal income tax

Taxable income runs through the 2026 brackets for your filing status, from 10% to 37%. Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket’s rate. The full tables are in the 2026 tax guide.

5. Quarterly payments

What you still owe, after W-2 withholding and payments you’ve already made, is split across the 2026 due dates that haven’t passed yet. Late in the year that means one or two larger payments instead of four.

The calculator also shows the least you can pay to avoid an underpayment penalty. That’s the smaller of 90% of this year’s tax or 100% of last year’s (110% if last year’s adjusted gross income was over $150,000). If you expect to owe less than $1,000 after withholding, you usually don’t need estimated payments at all.

What it doesn’t include

State and local income tax, itemized deductions, tax credits such as the child tax credit, capital gains, and the extra limits on the business income deduction above $201,750 of taxable income ($403,500 joint). The calculator tells you when you’re near that line.
