Home office deduction: simplified or regular method?

There are two ways to claim a home office. One takes five minutes, the other can be worth more. Here’s how to choose.

By taxmetter team September 25, 2026 2 min read

In this article
  1. Do you qualify?
  2. The simplified method
  3. The regular method
  4. Which is better?
  5. Try it with your numbers

If you work from home, part of the cost of your home may be a business expense. The IRS gives you two ways to claim it.

Do you qualify?

The space must be used regularly and exclusively for your business. A spare room that is only your office qualifies. A kitchen table you also eat at doesn’t. It must also be your main place of business, or a place you regularly meet clients.

The simplified method

  • Deduct $5 for every square foot of office space
  • Up to 300 square feet, so a maximum of $1,500 a year
  • No receipts for rent, utilities or repairs needed

A 120 sq ft office gives a $600 deduction. The deduction can’t be more than your business profit, so it can’t create a loss.

The regular method

Work out the share of your home used for business, for example 150 sq ft of a 1,500 sq ft home is 10%. Then deduct that share of your actual costs:

  • Rent, or mortgage interest and depreciation if you own
  • Utilities, home insurance and repairs
  • Property tax

Expenses only for the office itself, like painting that room, can be deducted in full.

Which is better?

The regular method is often worth more if you rent in an expensive area, or your office is larger than 300 sq ft. The simplified method wins on time, record-keeping and simplicity, and it avoids depreciation questions if you later sell a home you own.

A quick test: multiply your yearly rent and utilities by your office percentage. If the result is well above your simplified figure, the regular method may be worth the extra paperwork.
