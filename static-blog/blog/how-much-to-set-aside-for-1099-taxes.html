How much should I set aside for taxes on 1099 income?

Setting aside 20–30% is a good start, but your real number depends on your income, expenses and filing status. Here’s how to work it out.

By taxmetter team October 23, 2026 2 min read

In this article

  2. The two taxes you owe
  3. A worked example
  4. What changes your number

When you’re paid on a 1099, nobody takes tax out of your pay. The money lands in your account in full, and it’s up to you to keep enough back for the IRS. Save too little and April hurts. Save too much and you’re short of cash all year.

The quick answer

Most freelancers with a moderate income should set aside between 20% and 30% of every 1099 payment for federal tax. Add your state’s income tax on top if you have one.

That range is wide because two different taxes are involved, and they work in different ways.

The two taxes you owe

  • Self-employment tax is 15.3% on 92.35% of your net profit. It pays for Social Security and Medicare, and it applies from the first dollar of profit (once your net earnings reach $400).
  • Federal income tax uses the same brackets as everyone else. You only pay it on income above your standard deduction ($16,100 for a single filer in 2026), so the first part of your profit is income-tax free.

Because self-employment tax is a flat rate and income tax is progressive, your set-aside percentage rises as your income rises.

A worked example

Say you’re single, earned $60,000 on 1099-NEC forms in 2026, and had no expenses.

  • Self-employment tax: $60,000 × 92.35% × 15.3% = $8,478
  • Income tax, after deducting half of the self-employment tax, the $16,100 standard deduction and the 20% business income deduction: $3,559
  • Total: $12,037, or about 20% of what you earned

Paid in four equal installments, that’s about $3,009 a quarter.

What changes your number

  • Expenses. Every dollar of business expenses lowers both taxes. At this income, $1,000 of expenses saves about $230.
  • A day job. W-2 wages fill up the lower income tax brackets first, so your 1099 income is taxed at a higher rate.
  • Filing status. Married couples filing jointly get a bigger standard deduction and wider brackets.
  • Retirement savings. SEP-IRA or solo 401(k) contributions lower income tax (but not self-employment tax).

A simple habit that works

Open a separate savings account just for tax. Every time a client pays you, move your percentage across straight away. When a quarterly due date comes round, the money is already waiting.
